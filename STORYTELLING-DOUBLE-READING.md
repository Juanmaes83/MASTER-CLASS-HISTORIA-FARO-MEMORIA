## Core idea
The experience must work simultaneously as:

1. A literal story about an Atlantic lighthouse on its final night.
2. A human story about depression, loneliness, loss of orientation and the importance of sending a signal / asking for help when lost.

The second meaning should not be explained at the beginning. It should emerge progressively and retrospectively reframe the entire experience.

## Narrative progression

### 00 — Opening / Distance
Literal: a lighthouse preparing for its final rotation.
Human: from far away, it is difficult to know how much someone is carrying.

### 01 — Coast / Distance
Literal: weather, salt, oxidation, endurance.
Human: standing does not mean untouched.

### 02 — Keeper / Solitude
Literal: an empty keeper's room, routine, absence.
Human: solitude accumulates quietly in routines and spaces.

### 02.5 — Ascent / Effort
Literal: the stair narrows as the visitor climbs.
Human: the next step matters when the whole climb feels impossible.

### 03 — Machine / What nobody sees
Literal: the Fresnel and clockwork reveal the hidden complexity behind the light.
Human: what appears effortless from far away may require invisible effort to maintain.

Key line:
> The lens does not make light. It teaches light where to go.

Human reading: help does not have to create a person's light; sometimes it helps them find direction for what is still there.

### 04 — Beam / Signal
Literal: the lighthouse sends its coded signal across the coast.
Human: asking for help is a signal, not a failure.

Key idea:
> I am here. Look this way.

### 05 — Afterlight / Answer
Literal: the lighthouse stops shining but remains a coordinate, memory and guide.
Human: value, connection and identity do not disappear when someone can no longer shine as before.

Primary line:
> A lighthouse can stop shining before it stops guiding.

Closing ideas:
> Being lost is not the same as being unreachable.

> Even a lighthouse needs someone watching for its signal.

## Structural language
**LIGHT → SIGNAL → ANSWER**

Spanish:
**LUZ → SEÑAL → RESPUESTA**

## Tone rules
- Never romanticize depression or suffering.
- Never imply that isolation is noble or necessary.
- Do not make the metaphor clinical or diagnostic.
- Do not reveal the second reading too early.
- Do not turn the final section into a generic motivational message.
- Keep the lighthouse story fully coherent even for a user who never reads the human metaphor.
- The emotional resolution must include connection and help-seeking, not only melancholy.
