01 The Coast

Before a lighthouse is a tower, it is a promise across distance.

The sea does not know the building is obsolete.

It keeps arriving in the same long lines, grinding salt into iron and limestone. On the final evening the tower is still white enough to catch the remaining sky, but the lantern above it is already dark.

02 The Keeper

A room can remember a person without showing their face.

Oilskin. Brass. A cup gone cold. One final line in the log.

The keeper is never shown. Presence is reconstructed from pressure marks, salt on cloth, repeated measurements, and the small discipline of objects returned to the same place every night.

03 The Machine

The lens does not make light. It teaches light where to go.

Glass becomes architecture. Rotation becomes time.

A Fresnel lens compresses a massive curved optic into concentric prisms. Behind it, gearing turns an ordinary source into a coded signal visible beyond the horizon. This is the mechanical heart of the story — and our vertical slice.

04 The Beam

For six seconds, the whole coast knows exactly where it is.

The final rotation begins.

The beam cuts through suspended salt and fog, not as spectacle but as function. One sweep, darkness, one sweep again. The scroll now controls azimuth: the visitor is no longer reading about the signal — they are turning it.

05 · Afterlight

A lighthouse can stop shining before it stops guiding.

At dawn the lens is only glass again. The beam is gone. What remains is a coordinate, a building, and the memory of every person who once looked for that pulse in bad weather.
